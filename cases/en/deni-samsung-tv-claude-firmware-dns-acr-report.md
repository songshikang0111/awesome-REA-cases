# Deni's Samsung TV: Claude compares firmware with 24 hours of traffic before DNS ad/ACR blocking

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-16
Stage: Needs verification

At 01:29:51 Beijing time on November 16, 2026, Deni (@0xDeniAi) reports using Claude Code on their Samsung TV. It allegedly obtained the official update for the exact model and mapped outbound services, then captured 24 hours of TV traffic to distinguish services present in firmware from those actually communicating. The goal is removing home-screen ads and ACR (automatic content recognition) viewing tracking while retaining updates, applications and streaming.

The author says only the first group was blocked at DNS level, without flashing, bricking or remaining ads. These are unilateral results, with no TV model, firmware version, service/domain inventory, capture, DNS rules, before/after tests or reboot-persistence evidence. They do not prove all tracking stopped, streaming is wholly unaffected or the approach works across Samsung TVs.

The author calls their intervention to get Claude to continue W.H.I.P., Weighted Human Intervention Protocol, and asks whether to open-source it. That name and promotional framing are not an audited general protocol, released source or a guaranteed way around refusals. REA engine use is not explicit, so classification is other-ai. Retrieval confirms attribution, text and a video attachment only. This collection did not watch it, download firmware, connect a TV or apply blocking rules; status remains unverified.
