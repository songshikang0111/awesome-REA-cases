# Eight cinematic camera moves into AI prompts: Wujie AI's camera-trajectory reconstruction thread

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-19
Stage: Needs verification

At 14:19:38 Beijing time on November 19, 2026, Wujie AI (formerly Maze Guru, @mazeguru_ai) says it analyzed and reverse-engineered eight iconic Hollywood camera moves into production-ready AI video prompts. The goal is not recovering film project files, but avoiding static camera movement in generated videos. It also proposes uploading hard-to-describe reference clips to Wujie AI for trajectory analysis and prompt conversion.

Supplementary same-author posts at 14:19:40–14:19:49 list Hitchcock's dolly zoom, Michael Bay's hero orbit, Fincher's impossible tracking shot, Tarantino's trunk POV, Cuarón's immersive long take, Wong Kar-wai's step-printed motion blur, Kubrick's symmetrical corridor tracking and Scorsese's Steadicam venue pass-through. They go beyond style names: the hero orbit specifies 10 seconds, 320 degrees clockwise, knee-to-chest elevation and deceleration in the final two seconds; the Fincher example divides a continuous keyhole-to-banquet-hall trajectory into intervals and prohibits clipping through solid objects; the Wong example describes 12fps/6fps duplication and slow-shutter trails.

These numbers are generation instructions, not measured original-film parameters or output measurements. The first example's tags say dolly-in/zoom-out, whereas its body says dolly-back/zoom-in, an internal directional inconsistency that prevents treating it as precise photographic recovery. Each example has video attached, but none was watched, frame-compared or generated here. Source-film inventories, error metrics and original prompts are absent. The eight examples form one style-research project, not eight independent results. Retained as an unverified report of broader AI-assisted visual reconstruction; REA is unmentioned.

November 19, 2026 main-post candidate snapshot: 1 like, 62 views, 0 reposts, 2 replies. Supplementary posts explain the project on the same date; they are not extra candidates or independent validation.
