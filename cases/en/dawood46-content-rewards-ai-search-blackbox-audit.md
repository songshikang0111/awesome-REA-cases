# How AI search cites Content Rewards: dawood46's eighteen-question black-box audit

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-23
Stage: Analysis test

At 05:49:47 Beijing time on November 23, 2026, dawood46 (@notdwd) reports studying how clipping-marketing platform Content Rewards appears in AI search, crediting @wespreadjam for the system. The method tests eighteen buyer-intent questions across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI search, counts cited answers and inspects articles, campaign pages and external recommendations. Retained as broader AI black-box behavior research, not software decompilation or recovery of model weights or internal ranking algorithms.

The author reports visibility scores of 43.3 for Content Rewards, 9.7 for Ssemble and 8.3 for Whop, with the site cited in twenty-two of forty-five answers containing sources. About one hundred articles were reportedly published November 9–15, out of 135 total, while about sixty irrelevant older posts were removed. Questions comprise 36% of subheadings and median article length is about 1,480 words. One article uses data from 707 clipping campaigns and reports a median price of one dollar per thousand views; ChatGPT and Google AI reportedly cited it for CPM questions. The author also reports 762 campaign pages with few citations, versus ten homepage appearances and four each for a platform-selection article and the about page. Articles, original data and competitor recommendations appeared more often than campaign listings in the tested answers.

Reported gaps include continued association with Whop in eighteen of twenty Gemini/Google AI answers naming the brand, versus one of seven ChatGPT answers. The author says the company became independent in 2025 but still uses a Whop address and payments; llms.txt and explanatory pages did not prevent confusion. None of the three models reportedly cited the platform for fake-view detection or sponsored TikTok-view verification questions despite it offering that feature. These are observations on finite questions, not general error rates or proof that particular page types cause rankings.

The opening claims of sevenfold AI traffic, 25,400 visits in one day and tripled signups in seven days are the author's business-promotion figures, not causal results established by this audit. Retrieving the original confirms complete text, attribution and a photo attachment. The image, question list, model versions, timing configuration, raw answers, score formula, website history and traffic dashboard were not reviewed. Figures were not independently recalculated; analysis-test denotes the author's reported analysis, not reproduction here. No audit or target was run; REA is unmentioned.
