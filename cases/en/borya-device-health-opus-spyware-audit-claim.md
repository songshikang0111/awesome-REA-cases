# Device Health: Borya claims Opus analyzed a suspicious APK on his sister's phone

Category: Applications and clients · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-10-30
Stage: Needs verification

At 17:31:52 Beijing time on October 30, 2026, Borya | Official (@BoryaOfficial) claims that, after his sister's phone ran hot and died by 3 pm, he connected a laptop, enabled USB debugging and gave Claude Code with Opus 5.5 one prompt. The post claims a three-minute inventory of 71 processes: 67 normal, three old games tracking location and one Device Health app without an app-drawer icon. It allegedly had accessibility, photo/file, microphone, location, SMS and device-administrator access.

The author further claims five minutes of APK reversing found new photos uploaded within 45 seconds, over 2,700 photos already sent, keylogging, screenshots when Instagram or a banking app opened, GPS logging every ten minutes and zipped uploads every 20 minutes. The account ends with a wipe, password changes and 2FA, and 35% battery remaining at 11 pm. All timings, counts, behaviors and battery figures are narrative claims without a package name, hash, Android version, decompilation, packet capture, permission screenshots or retest log. Lookup did not supply those missing materials.

Device Health is merely the reported display name, not an identified malware family or a basis for labeling legitimate similarly named system apps spyware. The post is marked as a quote; the first-person narrative's original tester and video were not independently traced. Improved battery life after a wipe would not prove all upload or keylogging inferences. This collection accessed neither a phone, APK nor exfiltration server. Retained as an unverified security-analysis report, not a detection guarantee or confirmed disclosure.
