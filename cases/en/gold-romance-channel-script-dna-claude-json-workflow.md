(@thegoldeenhand) says a four-minute study of a romance channel reportedly earning $38,000 per month found narrative structure—not free TTS, visuals or upload frequency—to be decisive. The proposed method selects the three highest-viewed videos rather than the latest three and listens to their first two minutes for crisis/mystery/status-disruption openings, tension/relief oscillation and emotional-peak placement.

The full post supplies the proposed Claude JSON fields: hook type; context and characters in minutes 1–5; rising conflict and breathing room in minutes 5–15; a climax in minutes 15–18 of a 20-minute video or 28–35 of a 40-minute video; and resolution plus thematic connection in the final 2–5 minutes. Topics, people and settings change while structure remains. The author promotes TubeChef for automating much of the encoding and an Elevate course at $200 per month. This records observable content-pattern inference and an AI-generation workflow, not recovery of software or a recommendation algorithm.

The channel is unnamed; no three samples, full JSON, Claude revision, generated script, revenue records, retention curves or controlled comparisons are supplied.
