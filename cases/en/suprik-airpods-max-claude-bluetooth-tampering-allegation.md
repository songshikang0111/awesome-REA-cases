# A second-hand AirPods Max allegation: Suprik says Claude found abnormal Bluetooth identity and a recording app

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-04
Stage: Needs verification

At 19:42:30 Beijing time on November 4, 2026, Suprik (@Supriik_) says his second-hand AirPods Max appeared normal. After writing about hidden cameras, he asked Claude to inspect six laptop Bluetooth devices: five were reportedly clean, while the headphones showed firmware absent from Apple's release list, an extra keyboard identity and microphone activity for 14 hours daily. He also claims an unrecognized BTAudioSync application appeared two days after pairing, producing 1,284 clips and 63 hours of audio over five weeks with 41 uploads to an unfamiliar server at 3am.

He attributes this to a tampered individual second-hand device, explicitly not Apple surveillance. He says Claude helped in one evening to disconnect and forget the headphones, remove the app and launch mechanisms, block uploads, delete clips, tighten microphone permissions, reset Bluetooth and preserve evidence. This is retained as a concrete AI-assisted security-investigation claim, not a confirmed hardware implant or reproducible vulnerability.
