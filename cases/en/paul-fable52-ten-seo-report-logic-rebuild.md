# Rebuilding ten paid SEO report types: Paul uses Fable 5.2 and real data on his ChatSEO site

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-13
Stage: Analysis test

At 18:35:11 Beijing time on November 13, 2026, Paul Vengeons (@VengeonsP) publishes an article reporting reconstruction of ten paid SEO report types in one Fable 5.2 agent session. He identifies himself as an SEO consultant and ChatSEO co-founder, using his own chatseo.app as the test site rather than exposing client data; the article also promotes his product. The target is separating and rebuilding existing SEO reports' data layer and their filtering, joining and sorting logic, not reversing Ahrefs/Semrush binaries or copying proprietary databases.

He says the data were read on November 13: 28-day Search Console exports, DataForSEO keywords/backlinks/live US results and PageSpeed Insights. The ten types are site audit, keyword gap, backlink gap, competitor top pages, traffic estimate, content score, on-page check, keyword cannibalization, internal-link opportunities and rank report. The data bill for one site and competitor set was reportedly under $1, including five cents for the keyword gap and three cents for the backlink gap. This excludes model/subscription and setup costs and does not establish other sites' costs. Of 2,249 gap keywords, he reports reviewing the first 300 by volume and retaining 58 with business intent. Of 413 multi-URL queries, normalization of anchors and language versions leaves 145; his reading identifies one same-type article pair worth considering for a merge.

The article distinguishes estimates from observations: an estimated 150 monthly US visits versus 269 US Search Console clicks over 28 days, with 1,850 from France. A US database estimate is not total site traffic. A page scored 97 but received three clicks from 245,000 impressions in its keyword family. Its exact query had 38,600 impressions, two clicks and average position nine, yet the live US search that day did not show it in the first 18 results. Averages hide time variation, not its cause.

The author explicitly did not compare rebuilt tables line by line against paid suites, test other models, crawl an entire site or execute the suggested link, title and article-merge changes. There is no before/after traffic improvement. A referenced older 103K-click screenshot cannot be attributed to this run's unimplemented actions. All figures are a single-site, single-day author report; raw exports and agent logs were not audited, independently reproduced or obtained through paid API calls here.
