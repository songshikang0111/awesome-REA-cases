# Routing a 400-function app between Grok and Fable: separating an older roughly $5 estimate from a later $3.10 claim

Category: Applications and clients · REA
Record date: 2026-11-17
Stage: Analysis test

At 19:40:11 Beijing time on November 13, 2026, @starmexxx publishes five configurations combining Fable 5.2, Grok 4.6 and REA. The readable article claims five paid apps mapped that week for $14 total, but provides concrete process figures for only one unnamed 400-function menu-bar app. This remains one low-detail analysis experiment, without inventing the other four targets. Grok alone reportedly took 41 minutes and $1.80, handled plumbing accurately but struggled with two mathematical functions. In the hybrid, Grok labels functions and uncertainty goes to Fable: 37 of 400 were escalated, the other 363 described as plumbing.

The older article estimates the hybrid at about $5 with Fable-level results for roughly one third of the price. These are author estimates and judgments without per-function answers, match definitions, accuracy, bills or a controlled baseline. The five-app $14 total is not this hybrid's cost; eight-worker and full-lab routes are setup suggestions. Comments about other games, model prices, REA capabilities and Craft Anything are not verified facts about this test.

At 01:56:18 on November 17, Ridark (@ridark_eth) reports adding a 0.8 confidence gate between Grok and Fable. REA supplies pseudocode per function; Grok returns JSON with a one-line purpose, confidence and reason for doubt. Above 0.8 the result stands; below it Fable 5.2 maps inputs, outputs and side effects and tags observation/inference/unknown. Of 400 functions, 363 stay on the cheap route and 37 escalate. The source does not specify handling of exactly 0.8. Ridark lists Grok-only at 41 minutes/$1.80/19 unknowns, Fable-only at 26 minutes/$9.40/4 unknowns and hybrid at 33 minutes/$3.10/5 unknowns, claiming a 67% bill reduction. This differs from the older approximately $5 estimate. Without conditions and bills, it is not a confirmed correction of the old experiment, controlled replication or accuracy improvement; fewer unknowns does not establish correctness.

At 02:17:25, Mojunel.eth (@mojunel) reports the same 400-function, 41-minute/$1.80 and five-app/week/$14 figures, describing CLI registration of agents, doctor engine checks, Ghidra for native binaries and direct scripts for Electron. The post traces click/handler/IPC/network paths, ranks functions by call count and writes a top-30 research Markdown with evidence IDs, versioned per build. One function per six seconds, under half a cent per call and evidence for every call are this post's claims; functions and calls are not identical billing units.

Retrieval confirms both later accounts and wording. Their similar numbers lack app identity, version, report, scripts, capture logs and per-function answers; independent experiments, a shared run or common authorship are not established. Evidence is appended to the original target rather than opening duplicate entries, with separate attribution. Videos were not examined, REA executed, models configured or bills checked. Status remains analysis-test.
