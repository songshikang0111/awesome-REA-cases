# From a SPIR-V research idea to recovering his own shaders: Sebastian Aaltonen reports identical output

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-24
Stage: In progress

At 22:06:45 Beijing time on November 8, 2026, Sebastian Aaltonen (@SebAaltonen) discusses GPU-shader reverse engineering. He says recent AI-written PS5 emulators translate AMD RDNA instructions into SPIR-V, and suggests analysts reuse open tools, translate further into HLSL, GLSL or Metal, then ask AI to name variables. The goal is to turn lower-level GPU instructions into readable expressions for understanding existing rendering logic.

The November 8 post was only an idea with a concrete conversion chain. It supplied no emulator repository, revision, decompiler, shader sample, output, naming evaluation or semantic-equivalence test. AI authorship of emulators was also an attributed claim. Retrieval on November 9 confirmed attribution and wording. Translation and readable naming do not recover original source, original variable names or a whole graphics pipeline.

At 06:24:16 Beijing time on November 24, during a GTA 6 PC discussion, he additionally says he used AI to reverse engineer all his own shaders, producing identical SPIRV and clean code. Retrieval confirms attribution and text, but there is no named game, sample count, input/output, hash, compiler options, model, repository or comparison log. All refers only to his unspecified collection. Clean is subjective, and identical output is his report, not a byte comparison performed here. It is not established that the experiment used every step proposed on November 8, nor does it demonstrate complete PS5 compatibility or a GTA 6 port.

His call for a Q1 2027 PC launch and a date announcement before Christmas is advice to a publisher, not an official schedule or reversing duration. This entry is updated from a pure idea to an in-progress author experiment report. No tool was downloaded or run and no shader independently recompiled here; REA remains unmentioned.
