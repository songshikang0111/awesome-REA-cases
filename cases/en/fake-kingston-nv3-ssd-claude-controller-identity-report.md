# Identifying a fake Kingston NV3: a report of Claude's initial misdiagnosis and later controller checks

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-17
Stage: Needs verification

At 01:45:01 Beijing time on November 17, 2026, Vaibhav Sisinty (@VaibhavSisinty) relays a Reddit user's SSD purchase. Having ordered 2TB, the user received a drive displaying 4TB and thought it was a lucky upgrade. Windows and SMART reported healthy. Claude Code reportedly spent hours scanning, formatting and retrying smaller batches while continuing to blame bad hardware. After the user questioned whether it really was Drive D and requested research, Claude queried the device's reported identity.

The post says its controller was Realtek rather than the Silicon Motion it expected in a genuine Kingston NV3, its serial carried a Realtek identifier and firmware matched no genuine NV3. The 4TB-labeled drive allegedly held about 256GB. This is related AI-assisted hardware identity and fault analysis, not SSD-firmware decompilation or public verification of a genuine production batch's controller. Model identity and counterfeit conclusions stay attributed to the secondary source; a controller brand alone is not supplied as a universal authenticity rule.

Retrieval confirms the poster, wording and an image attachment. The original Reddit post, SMART output, controller IDs, firmware, serial and capacity tests were not read, nor was the image inspected. Hours is not a work log; about 256GB was not independently measured. Claude's early misdiagnoses and the human challenge are retained rather than presenting autonomous one-pass counterfeit detection. No disk was connected or formatted here. Status remains unverified.
