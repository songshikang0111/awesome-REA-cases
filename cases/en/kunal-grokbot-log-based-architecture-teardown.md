# Inside Grok Bot: Kunal studies persistent machines, workers and approvals through his own bot logs

Category: Applications and clients · REA use unconfirmed
Record date: 2026-11-13
Stage: Related project

At 15:25:10 Beijing time on November 13, 2026, Kunal Malhotra (@malhotrak_) shares Inside Grok Bot: the whole machine. The candidate contains only an article link; supplementary public article data supplies the text. He says he has run about 40 Grok Bots daily since late August and uses his own logs to study system components, persistent cloud computers, long jobs, memory and approvals. It is described as part one of a five-part teardown. This is a related AI-system behavioral study, not a claim to xAI internal source or completed binary decompilation. AI assistance in the analysis is unspecified and REA is unmentioned.

The stated material comprises 290 chat turns across 20 bots in a September snapshot, 1,541 background transcripts (about 1,370 routine runs and 170 workers), 5,074 timestamped audit events from September 1 to 17, 10,062 readings from a week-long collector, and memory files from 16 bots plus 119 older facts available to his main bot. These historical materials and a November 10 chat support the current article; they are not experiments newly generated in this window. The full raw data was not audited here.

The author describes a shared persistent machine per account with separate bot chats and memories, connector/shell-first tool selection before browsers/UI, and a documentation-described separate Auto Review model for risky actions that does not cover memory writes. His log statistics report intent sentences in 72% of tool-using turns, a median of five tool calls and two short messages, and worker launches in 31% of turns. A currency collector reportedly used a 222-line Python script for about a week, producing 10,062 readings with zero errors. Two collectors had 1,329 health checks, at least 890 silent endings and one warning after three consecutive 401s. Zero errors in the readings is not zero interruption; a later passage also mentions one gap.

He reports 80 profile facts and 206 monthly-log lines across 16 bots, with corrections appended as dated lines. Failures include an unsolicited X introduction post, deleted before a per-action approval rule was added to memory. This still depends on model compliance, not hard permissions. A 30-day $1,000 revenue experiment had a $100 soft cap, $200 hard cap and questions required above $25. It produced three products, 11 emails, three bounces, one spam complaint and $0 revenue; this does not demonstrate tool-enforced budget limits.

The article explicitly treats the memory writer, front-agent/worker implementation and sleep explanation as inference or secondary evidence rather than confirmed internals. It cites xAI writing, documentation, OpenCompany and an Instinct teardown. Those links were not individually verified, images inspected, logs recalculated or bots run here. Available observations and limitations are retained as related-project; studying an AI product is not relabeled as confirmed AI-assisted reverse engineering.
