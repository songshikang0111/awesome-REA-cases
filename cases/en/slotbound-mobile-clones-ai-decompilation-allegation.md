# From Slotbound's PC demo to mobile lookalikes: MrWhite relays an unproven AI-decompilation allegation

Category: Games and preservation · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-24
Stage: Needs verification

At 19:45:17 Beijing time on November 24, 2026, MrWhite (@neuronxwhite) relays the Slotbound creator's belief that two mobile lookalikes were made by AI decompiling a PC demo without source access. Supplementary posts from 19:45:16 to 19:45:20 identify Slotbound: allegedly less than two months after its July demo, a paid Douyin minigame copied its UI and art, followed in October by WeChat's Siluotuo Zhanji (斯洛托战记). These are grouped as allegations about two imitations of the same demo target, without inferring the same developer or proven AI use from visual similarity.

The thread attributes the account to GameLook and says Chinese players had both lookalikes removed after reports, while the original creator lacked a Chinese phone number to complain directly. It also mentions a November 13 Steam release, 200,000 sales in six days, 100,000 wishlists and v1.0.3. These are secondary-report market and enforcement figures. Store data, takedown notices and sales were not checked here, and commercial success is not evidence of a reversing method.

The GameLook article, original creator's technical analysis, clone source, binaries and AI invocation records were not obtained; the supplementary source link's body was unavailable. Decompilation, visual reconstruction, asset copying and other methods cannot be distinguished. Only the creator's suspicion as relayed by MrWhite is recorded, not proven AI decompilation or a legal infringement finding. Neither the original game nor lookalikes were downloaded.
