# Kapsys SmartVision3: a reported screen-reader launcher rebuild after AI review of decompiled system files

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-11-10
Stage: Needs verification

At 00:44:20 Beijing time on November 10, 2026, Yewen Jiu (@fengxi5678) describes modifying a Kapsys SmartVision3 accessibility keypad phone. A developer reportedly bought it second-hand for their visually impaired mother for 400 euros; it remained on Android 11 with April 2024 patches and frequent app failures. The post says the vendor claimed even Android 12 was impossible, whereas the developer installed Android 16 in two evenings. The stated workflow decompiled vendor system files for AI review, located spoken key handling deep in the system, installed a LineageOS 23.2 generic image and wrote a launcher reportedly using only 20MB of memory to restore speech. The mother reportedly still asks questions with the assistant key, without responses crashing midway.

Original-post retrieval confirms the reporter and wording. Although a developer blog is mentioned, the original author, blog URL, ROM, launcher source, AI model and debugging logs were not supplied. Version mapping, the two-evening duration, 20MB memory use and long-term stability were not checked. Nor were calling, physical keys, speech and emergency functions independently tested for completeness. This is a concrete accessibility-workflow reconstruction account, not a safe-upgrade guide for arbitrary SmartVision3 units or independent confirmation of vendor support policy. REA is unmentioned. No phone was flashed or accessibility testing performed here.
